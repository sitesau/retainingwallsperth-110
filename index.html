Retaining Walls Perth Glossary Explained

Retained ground means the soil held back above the lower level, not simply the visible face. A garden edge can be modest, while a split-level pad or boundary lift can change the design burden even when the front looks similar. The source guide frames Perth decisions around soil, slope, wall height and the permit rule, then asks whether the wall is near a boundary, fence, driveway, house pad or other work.

  • Measure fall across the area: do not price from one end only.
  • Note what sits close by: fences, services, trees and machinery access change scope.
  • Separate new work from repair: a failed wall may need investigation behind the face.

Permit terms to check before materials are chosen

The useful permit question is not whether the wall looks small. It is how much earth is held and whether the job connects with anything else. The cited WA position is narrow: low standalone retaining may sit under the exemption threshold, while work above 0.5 m, or linked to other building work, protection of adjoining land, a boundary wall or a dividing fence, needs closer review. The guide points readers to Building Regulations 2012 Schedule 4 and local government confirmation.

  • Exemption: do not assume a one-metre rule heard interstate applies.
  • AS 4678: where required, design should follow this retaining wall standard with permit and engineering sign-off.
  • Itemised scope: wall build, excavation, drainage, engineering and permit handling should appear as separate lines.

A quote that hides certification inside one headline number makes comparison harder, not cheaper.

Material names that sound similar but price differently

Limestone block is described as Perth's signature look, using cut Tamala limestone or reconstituted block; it often leads quotes when the wall is visible and conditions are simple. Concrete post and panel uses galvanised steel posts with slotted concrete panels, yet suppliers may also say precast or twin-side for similar systems, so check the build method rather than the label.

  • Besser block: linked in the guide to taller engineered retaining and rendered boundary finishes.
  • Timber sleeper: presented for garden terraces and low boundary retaining.
  • Rock and boulder: suited to sloped Perth Hills blocks where a battered, free-draining finish fits.

Coastal sand, inner sand belt and Darling Scarp foothills each shift the sensible shortlist because sand, slope, boundary pressure and winter runoff behave differently.

Drainage words that protect the wall and the budget

Drainage belongs inside the structure. The guide names ag-drain, gravel and geofabric behind every wall type and stresses Perth's wet winter and dry summer cycle. A cheap face rate can conceal thin backfill, poor pipe placement or no separation fabric, so ask where water will go, how layers are built and what is included in writing.

  • Ag-drain: collects water and gives it a path away from the face.
  • Geofabric: helps stop fines migrating into drainage layers.
  • Backfill: should be specified, not treated as generic fill.
  • Batter: a sloped face suited to some rock walls on hills blocks.

For repairs, look for lean, bulge, horizontal cracking, gaps at the base, seepage after rain or soil slumping above; fix the cause before patching the surface.

Quote phrases that need a follow-up question

Treat published rates as wall face guides only. The source gives limestone near $250 to $600 per square metre and concrete post and panel near $450 to $700, while site digging, water control and certification remain separate. If two prices sit far apart, the difference is often behind the wall: access, soil, disposal, drainage detail, engineering trigger or permit handling.

  • Per square metre: confirm whether it is face area, both sides or a whole system.
  • Supply and install: ask what is excluded, such as excavation or disposal.
  • Engineering included: request the deliverable, review stage and who lodges paperwork.
  • Like for like: compare only after height, length, drainage and permit lines match.

Homeowners across coastal suburbs, the sand plain and foothills can use the same glossary, then adjust examples to their own measurements and local government advice.

  1. Record the site. Write approximate height and length, slope direction, boundary proximity and machinery access.
  2. Map the water path. Check where winter rain ponds, then ask how ag-drain, gravel and geofabric will be placed.
  3. Confirm the permit position. Ask local government about the 0.5 m threshold, linked building work and adjoining land issues.
  4. Compare itemised quotes. Request separate prices for wall construction, excavation, drainage, engineering and permit handling.

Common questions

Is 0.5 m a guaranteed permit-free height in WA? No. The guidance describes a narrow exemption position for low work that is not connected to other building work, protection of adjoining land, a boundary wall or a dividing fence. Confirm with local government before work starts.

Why can similar-looking quotes differ so much? Face rates often leave out access, excavation, disposal, drainage layers, engineering and permit handling. The source guide says the hidden detail behind the wall usually explains the gap.

Which material should a Perth homeowner shortlist first? Start with site facts, not taste. Limestone suits many visible local walls, concrete post and panel is a system to compare by construction method, Besser suits some engineered or rendered cases, timber fits low garden work, and rock suits battered hills sites.
